My research focuses on climate extremes and wildfires, examining how these events emerge, evolve, and affect people and ecosystems. I connect climate science, wildfire science, and data-driven approaches to investigate their mechanisms and spatiotemporal patterns.

My published work examines the persistence and synchronicity of extreme fire weather and their relationships with wildfire activity. I also develop open datasets and tools for studying heatwaves and compound climate events, providing reproducible foundations for analysing extremes across time and space.

I combine Earth observations, hydroclimatic analysis, statistics, and data-driven methods to investigate the mechanisms and consequences of extreme events. My current research explores extreme wildfire behavior, environmental drivers, societal impacts, and approaches to anticipating future risks.
